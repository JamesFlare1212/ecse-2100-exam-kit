--- PAGE 10 ---
 Why Study Electromagnetism (E&M)?

•  Electromagnetism is the most fundamental theory of electrical engineering:
   the one that tells us how electric magnetic fields and related concepts (such as
   current and voltage) actually work and relate to each other.

•  This means that by understanding E&M, you will also understand how electrical
  systems work at a fundamental level.

•  You will also understand the limits of other theories such as circuit theory (i.e.
  where these theories break down).

--- PAGE 11 ---
Why Study Electromagnetism (E&M)?
  Relationship with Circuit Theory

  Circuit theory uses simplified (lumped) models of components. The model,
 however, does not include:

   •  Details on how the components actually work

   •  Distributed properties (such as transmission lines)

   •  Electromagnetic waves (such as microwaves, radio waves, and optics)

   •  Noise

     • ground returns

Crosstalk: when electromagnetic signals from one circuit or transmission line interfere with nearby
circuits or transmission lines

Crosstalk occurs due to the mutual capacitance and inductance between conductors, which allows
for the transfer of electromagnetic energy between them.

Why Study Electromagnetism (E&M)?

Electromagnetic wave propagation devices, such as antennas, fundamentally
cannot be described using traditional circuit theory.

We must take into account the geometry of the device, the materials and
propagation media involved, and - most importantly - to think of electric
phenomena as being composed of waves.

Back to electrical engineering. What does this all mean?
   • When you have voltage (V), you have an electric field intensity (E in V/m)
   • When you have current (I), you have a magnetic flux density (B in Wb/m2).

 Also:
   • When you have a change in B, a voltage is generated
   • When you have a change in E, a current is generated

Transmission Line History

    longer cables

 •  First working undersea telegraph line laid across the

     •  Voltage drop due to resistance
     •  Signal delay
     •  Signal distortion

Transmission Line Model

 •  The purpose of a transmission line is to transmit a signal and/or power from
   one place to another.
 •  Examples include:
     •  Power Lines (60Hz)
     •  Coaxial Cables
     •  Twisted Pairs
     •  Interconnects inside a computer

 •  Note that these all have two conductors.
 •  In transmission line theory, we represent a transmission line as having certain
    properties per unit length.
 •  Under certain conditions, we can model a short section of transmission line using
    circuit elements like resistors, inductors and capacitors.
