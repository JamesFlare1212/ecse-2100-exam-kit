Numerical Methods for Current

•  Since current density is proportional to (and parallel to) the electric field, we
  can represent Ԧ𝐽in finite element and finite difference analysis similarly to
  how we would represent 𝐸
    •  In finite difference, just multiply 𝐸by the 𝝈of the cell to get Ԧ𝐽



• To calculate resistance, note that we are addressing the flow of electrons
   (current) across equi-potentials (E field perpendicular to equipotential lines)

    •  In finite difference, calculate the total current 𝐼(from Ԧ𝐽) and divide by the
       potential difference 𝑉to get R for a geometry
