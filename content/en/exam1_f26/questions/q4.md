## Question

The load in the circuit below is to be matched to a $Z_0=50\Omega$ feedline using a quarter-wave transformer of characteristic impedance $Z_{0T}$ and a length of transmission line $d$ of characteristic impedance $Z_0$. The velocity factor of the transmission line of length $d$ is $0.85$ and the frequency of the signal on the line is $f=100\,\mathrm{MHz}=100\times10^6\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

## Guide

This problem first transforms a complex load to a real impedance, then completes the match with a quarter-wave transformer.

**Given：** The load is $Z_L=(150-j50)\,\Omega$ and the feedline is $50\,\Omega$. The section of length $d$ has velocity factor $0.85$ at $100\,\mathrm{MHz}$.

**Find：** Find the two real input impedances on the Smith chart, choose a branch for physical length and transformer impedance, and identify interfaces where reflections remain.

**Start here：** Find the real-axis crossings on the load's constant-reflection circle and convert wavelength readings to meters. The quarter-wave section performs the second transformation; a matched feedline does not imply that every internal interface is matched.

## Part a

Calculate the normalized load impedance. Plot it on the Smith chart.

### Solution

Use the normalized-impedance definition:

$$
z_L=\frac{Z_L}{Z_0}
$$

Given $Z_L=150-j50\,\Omega$ and $Z_0=50\,\Omega$, find $z_L$. Substitute and use the crib sheet’s [reflection coefficient](formula:reflection) to locate the Smith-chart point:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_L&=\frac{(150-j50)\,\Omega}{50\,\Omega}=\frac{150}{50}-j\frac{50}{50}=3-j,\\[4pt]
\Gamma_L&=\frac{z_L-1}{z_L+1}=\frac{2-j}{4-j}=\frac{(2-j)(4+j)}{(4-j)(4+j)}=\frac{9-2j}{17}
\end{aligned}
$$

Mark the intersection of the $r=3$ circle and $x=-1$ arc:

$$
z_L=\boxed{3-j}
$$

## Part b

What are the two values of purely real input impedance $Z_{in,d}$ that the transmission line of length $d$ could present? Show your work on the Smith chart. Give your impedances in unnormalized format.

### Solution

Use the crib sheet’s [reflection coefficient](formula:reflection) and solve for normalized impedance:

$$
\begin{aligned}\Gamma&=\frac{z-1}{z+1}\\\Gamma z+\Gamma&=z-1\\z-\Gamma z&=1+\Gamma\\(1-\Gamma)z&=1+\Gamma\\z&=\frac{1+\Gamma}{1-\Gamma}\end{aligned}
$$

Given $\Gamma_L=(9-2j)/17$ from (a), find both real input impedances. A lossless line preserves $|\Gamma|$; substitute the real-axis intersections $\Gamma=\pm\rho$:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle \rho=|\Gamma_L|=\left|\frac{9-2j}{17}\right|=\frac{\sqrt{9^2+2^2}}{17}=\frac{\sqrt{85}}{17}\approx0.542326\\[4pt]&\displaystyle r_{\min}=\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho},\qquad r_{\max}=\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluate the normalized resistances and multiply by $Z_0=50\,\Omega$:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle r_{\min}\approx0.2967425905,\qquad r_{\max}\approx3.3699240762\\[4pt]&\displaystyle R_{\min}=50(0.2967425905)\,\Omega,\qquad R_{\max}=50(3.3699240762)\,\Omega\end{aligned}
$$

This gives

$$
R_{\min}=\boxed{14.8371\,\Omega},\qquad R_{\max}=\boxed{168.4962\,\Omega}
$$

### Source correction

The official chart reads about $14.5\,\Omega$ and $170\,\Omega$ and writes “$0.26\times50=14.5$”, an inconsistent multiplication. Here the values are computed exactly from the given load.

## Part c

Choose one of the two values of $Z_{in,d}$ from part b). How long $d$ (in meters) must the transmission line be to present your chosen input impedance $Z_{in,d}$? Show your work on the Smith chart.

### Solution

Use the crib sheet’s [wavelength relation](formula:wave) and obtain the along-line [reflection coefficient](formula:reflection) from its [travelling-wave phasors](formula:phasor):

$$
\lambda=\frac uf,\qquad \Gamma_{\mathrm{in},d}=\Gamma_Le^{-2j\beta d},\quad\beta=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

Given velocity factor $0.85$, $f=100\,\mathrm{MHz}$ and the load reflection coefficient, find the distances to both real-axis intersections. First evaluate the wavelength and initial phase:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle \lambda=\frac{0.85(3\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s})}{100\times10^6\,\mathrm{Hz}}=2.55\,\mathrm m\\[4pt]&\displaystyle \theta_L=\arg(9-2j)=-\arctan(2/9)\approx-0.218669\,\mathrm{rad}\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute target phase $-\pi$ for the smaller resistance into the rotation relation. The other intersection is a further $\lambda/4$ toward the source:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle \theta_L-\frac{4\pi d_1}{\lambda}=-\pi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{d_1}{\lambda}=\frac{\pi+\theta_L}{4\pi}=0.232598878\\[4pt]&\displaystyle d_1=(0.232598878)(2.55)=0.593127\,\mathrm m,\qquad \frac{d_2}{\lambda}=0.232598878+\frac14=0.482598878\\[4pt]&\displaystyle d_2=(0.482598878)(2.55)=1.230627\,\mathrm m\end{aligned}
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{gathered}R_{\min}:\ d=\boxed{0.593127\,\mathrm m}\\ R_{\max}:\ d=\boxed{1.230627\,\mathrm m}\end{gathered}
$$

## Part d

For the case you chose in part c), find the characteristic impedance $Z_{0T}$ of the quarter-wave transformer that matches the load network to the $Z_0=50\Omega$ feedline.

### Solution

Use the [quarter-wave transformation](formula:quarter) from the crib sheet:

$$
Z_{\mathrm{in},T}=\frac{Z_{0T}^2}{R_{\mathrm{in},d}}
$$

Given the $50\,\Omega$ feedline and both real resistances from (b), find each $Z_{0T}$. Set the transformer input to $50\,\Omega$ and substitute each branch:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle 50\,\Omega=\frac{Z_{0T}^2}{R_{\mathrm{in},d}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z_{0T}=\sqrt{(50\,\Omega)R_{\mathrm{in},d}}\\[4pt]&\displaystyle Z_{0T,1}=\sqrt{50(14.8371295)}\,\Omega=\sqrt{741.856476}\,\Omega\approx27.2370\,\Omega\\[4pt]&\displaystyle Z_{0T,2}=\sqrt{50(168.4962038)}\,\Omega=\sqrt{8424.81019}\,\Omega\approx91.7868\,\Omega\end{aligned}
$$

Choose the value paired with the distance branch in (c):

$$
Z_{0T}=\boxed{27.2370\,\Omega\ \text{or}\ 91.7868\,\Omega}
$$

## Part e

Once the transmission line and load are matched to the feedline, maximum power will be transferred from the generator to the load in steady state. Where do reflections still occur in the system?
Circle all that apply.

a. At the interface between the feedline and the input to the $L=\lambda/4$ line.
b. At the interface between the $L=\lambda/4$ line and the input to the line of length $d$.
c. At the interface between the line of length $d$ and the load $Z_L$.
d. No reflections occur anywhere in the system since the feedline is matched to the load.

### Solution

Use the [local-interface reflection coefficient](formula:reflection) from the crib sheet:

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{right}}-Z_{\mathrm{left}}}{Z_{\mathrm{right}}+Z_{\mathrm{left}}}
$$

Given $Z_{0T}\ne50\,\Omega$ and $Z_L=150-j50\,\Omega$, identify the reflecting interfaces. Substitute the impedances at interfaces a, b and c:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle \Gamma_a^{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{Z_{0T}-50}{Z_{0T}+50}\ne0\\[4pt]&\displaystyle \Gamma_b^{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{50-Z_{0T}}{50+Z_{0T}}\ne0\\[4pt]&\displaystyle \Gamma_c=\frac{(150-j50)-50}{(150-j50)+50}=\frac{9-2j}{17}\ne0\end{aligned}
$$

The overall match from (d) cancels the phasors returning into the feedline, giving zero net input reflection alongside the local interface reflections:

$$
Z_{\mathrm{in,network}}=50\,\Omega,\qquad\Gamma_{\mathrm{feed,net}}=\frac{50-50}{50+50}=0
$$

This gives

$$
\boxed{\text{Interfaces (a), (b) and (c)}}
$$

Select a, b and c. Each impedance discontinuity has a local reflection; overall matching cancels the returning reflected phasors at the feedline, giving zero net input reflection.
