## Question

The coaxial cable to the right has a poor dielectric, which has allowed some charge to leak between the inner conductor and outer conductor. The cable has an inner conductor of radius $a$ with surface charge density $\rho_{sa}$ and an outer conducting shell of radius $b$, held at a potential $V(b)=0$. The region $a<r<b$ is filled with a dielectric of permittivity $\varepsilon$ and volumetric charge density given by:
$$\rho(r)=\rho_0\frac{a}{r}$$

## Guide

Cylindrical Gauss’s law, potential integration and differential capacitance.

**Given：** $a<r<b$, $\rho(r)=\rho_0a/r$, $\varepsilon$, $\rho_{sa}$ and $V(b)=0$.

**Find：** Find $\vec E(r)$, $V(r)$, $\rho_{sa}(V_0)$ and $C^\prime$.

**Start here：** Include both surface and volume charge; hold $\rho_0$ fixed when finding capacitance.

## Part a

Using Gauss’s law, find the electric field $\vec E(\vec r)$ in the region $a<r<b$. Include a diagram that shows the location of your Gaussian surface.

### Solution

Use [Gauss’s law](formula:gauss) and the [constitutive relation](formula:constitutive) from the crib sheet:

$$
\oiint_S\vec D\cdot d\vec S=\iiint\rho\,d\mathcal V=Q_{\mathrm{enc}},\qquad\vec D=\varepsilon\vec E
$$

Given $\rho(r)=\rho_0a/r$, $\rho_{sa}$ and $\varepsilon$, find $\vec E(r)$. Choose a coaxial Gaussian cylinder of radius $a<R<b$ and length $\ell$. A small side patch has area equal to its arc length $R\,d\phi$ times its height $dz$, directed along the outward normal $+\hat r$. Using $\hat r\cdot\hat r=1$, expand the dot product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\because\quad&\vec E(R)=E_r(R)\hat r,\quad d\vec S_{\mathrm{side}}=\hat r R\,d\phi\,dz,\quad\vec E\cdot d\vec S_{\mathrm{caps}}=0,\\
\therefore\quad&\Phi_D=\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}(\varepsilon E_r(R)\hat r)\cdot(\hat r R\,d\phi\,dz)\\
&\phantom{\Phi_D}={}\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}\varepsilon R E_r(R)(\hat r\cdot\hat r)\,d\phi\,dz\\
&\phantom{\Phi_D}={}\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}\varepsilon R E_r(R)\,d\phi\,dz
\end{aligned}
$$

Cylindrical symmetry makes $\varepsilon R E_r(R)$ independent of $\phi$ and $z$ at fixed $R$, so take it outside. Integrate over $\phi$ and evaluate its endpoints, then integrate over $z$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_D&=\varepsilon R E_r(R)\int_0^\ell\left(\int_0^{2\pi}1\,d\phi\right)dz\\
&=\varepsilon R E_r(R)\int_0^\ell[\phi]_0^{2\pi}\,dz\\
&=\varepsilon R E_r(R)\int_0^\ell(2\pi-0)\,dz\\
&=2\pi\varepsilon R E_r(R)\int_0^\ell1\,dz\\
&=2\pi\varepsilon R E_r(R)[z]_0^\ell\\
&=2\pi\varepsilon R E_r(R)(\ell-0)\\
&=2\pi R\ell\varepsilon E_r(R)
\end{aligned}
$$

Split the enclosed charge into surface and volume contributions. First integrate over the inner conductor; its surface radius is $a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{enc}}&=Q_{\mathrm{surface}}+Q_{\mathrm{volume}},\\
Q_{\mathrm{surface}}&=\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}\rho_{sa}a\,d\phi\,dz\\
&=a\rho_{sa}\int_0^\ell[\phi]_0^{2\pi}\,dz\\
&=a\rho_{sa}(2\pi-0)[z]_0^\ell\\
&=2\pi a\rho_{sa}(\ell-0)=2\pi a\ell\rho_{sa}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute the [cylindrical volume element](formula:geometry) and $\rho(r^\prime)=\rho_0a/r^\prime$ into the volume-charge integral. Cancel $1/r^\prime$ against $r^\prime$, then integrate over $r^\prime$, $\phi$ and $z$ in that order:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\because\quad&d\mathcal V=r^\prime\,dr^\prime\,d\phi\,dz,\quad\rho(r^\prime)=\frac{\rho_0a}{r^\prime},\\
\therefore\quad&Q_{\mathrm{volume}}=\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_a^R\frac{\rho_0a}{r^\prime}\,r^\prime\,dr^\prime\,d\phi\,dz\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}\rho_0a\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_a^R1\,dr^\prime\,d\phi\,dz\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}\rho_0a\int_0^\ell\!\int_0^{2\pi}[r^\prime]_a^R\,d\phi\,dz\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}\rho_0a(R-a)\int_0^\ell[\phi]_0^{2\pi}\,dz\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}\rho_0a(R-a)(2\pi-0)[z]_0^\ell\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}2\pi\rho_0a(R-a)(\ell-0)\\
&\phantom{Q_{\mathrm{volume}}}={}2\pi\ell\rho_0a(R-a)
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute the flux and enclosed charge into Gauss’s law, cancel $2\pi\ell$ and write $R=r$. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{enc}}&=2\pi a\ell\rho_{sa}+2\pi\ell\rho_0a(R-a)\\
&=2\pi a\ell[\rho_{sa}+\rho_0(R-a)],\\
2\pi R\ell\varepsilon E_r(R)&=2\pi a\ell[\rho_{sa}+\rho_0(R-a)],\\
E_r(R)&=\frac{a}{\varepsilon R}[\rho_{sa}+\rho_0(R-a)]
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\vec{E}(r)=\boxed{\frac{a}{\epsilon r}[\rho_{sa}+\rho_0(r-a)]\hat{r}}
$$

## Part b

Find the potential $V(\vec r)$ in the region $a<r<b$.

### Solution

Use the [potential-difference formula](formula:potential) from the crib sheet:

$$
V(b)-V(a)=-\int_a^b\vec E\cdot d\vec l
$$

Given $V(b)=0$, find $V(r)$. Substitute the field from (a), using the radial path $d\vec l=\hat r\,ds$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(r)&=-\int_b^r\vec E(s)\cdot(\hat r\,ds)=\int_r^b E_r(s)\,ds\\
&=\frac a\varepsilon\int_r^b\left[\frac{\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0}{s}+\rho_0\right]ds\\
&=\frac a\varepsilon\left[(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\int_r^b\frac1s\,ds+\rho_0\int_r^b1\,ds\right]\\
&=\frac a\varepsilon\left[(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\ln(s/a)+\rho_0s\right]_r^b\\
&=\frac a\varepsilon\left[(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\left(\ln\frac ba-\ln\frac ra\right)+\rho_0(b-r)\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Combine the logarithms using $\ln(b/a)-\ln(r/a)=\ln(b/r)$ to obtain

$$
V(r)=\boxed{\frac a\epsilon\left[(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\ln\frac br+\rho_0(b-r)\right]}
$$

## Part c

Find the potential difference between the conductors, $V_0=V(a)-V(b)$. Then, express the surface charge density on the inner conductor, $\rho_{sa}$, as a function of $V_0$ and $\rho_0$.

### Solution

Use the crib sheet’s [potential-difference formula](formula:potential) between the conductors:

$$
V_0=V(a)-V(b)=-\int_b^a\vec E\cdot d\vec l
$$

Given $V(b)=0$, find $V_0$ and $\rho_{sa}(V_0,\rho_0)$. Substitute $r=a$ into (b) and collect the $\rho_{sa}$ term:

$$
\begin{aligned}V_0&=\frac a\varepsilon\left[(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\ln(b/a)+\rho_0(b-a)\right],\\(\rho_{sa}-a\rho_0)\ln(b/a)&=\frac{\varepsilon V_0}{a}-\rho_0(b-a)\end{aligned}
$$

Solving for $\rho_{sa}$ gives

$$
\rho_{sa}=\boxed{\frac{\epsilon V_0}{a\ln(b/a)}+a\rho_0-\frac{\rho_0(b-a)}{\ln(b/a)}}
$$

## Part d

When a volumetric charge density exists in the region between two conductors, the capacitance cannot be computed using the usual formulas. Instead, it must be defined by $C=\frac{dQ}{dV_0}$, where $Q$ is the total charge on one conductor and $V_0$ is the potential difference between the conductors. Using this definition and your results from earlier parts, determine the capacitance per unit length $C^{\prime}$ of the cable.

### Solution

Use the differential-capacitance definition supplied in the question:

$$
C^\prime=\frac1\ell\left.\frac{\partial Q}{\partial V_0}\right|_{\rho_0}=\left.\frac{\partial Q^\prime}{\partial V_0}\right|_{\rho_0}
$$

With $\rho_0$ held fixed, find $C^\prime$. Substitute $\rho_{sa}$ from (c) into the inner conductor’s charge per unit length, $Q^\prime=2\pi a\rho_{sa}$:

$$
Q^\prime=2\pi a\rho_{sa}=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)}V_0+2\pi a\rho_0\left[a-\frac{b-a}{\ln(b/a)}\right]
$$

Differentiate with respect to $V_0$; the term containing $\rho_0$ is constant. This gives

$$
C^\prime=\boxed{\frac{2\pi\epsilon}{\ln(b/a)}}\quad [\mathrm{F/m}]
$$
