## Question

Shown below is a stack of three dielectric materials:
• Dielectric 1: occupies the region $y>0.01\,\mathrm{m}$ and has permittivity $\varepsilon_1=2\varepsilon_0$
• Dielectric 2: occupies the region $0<y<0.01\,\mathrm{m}$ and has permittivity $\varepsilon_2=5\varepsilon_0$
• Dielectric 3: occupies the region $y<0$ and has permittivity $\varepsilon_3=2\varepsilon_0$

The electric field in dielectric 1 is given by:
$$\vec E_1=100\hat x-100\hat y$$

## Guide

This problem applies tangential-field and normal-displacement boundary conditions to layered dielectrics and interprets them through polarization.

**Given：** The layers have permittivities $2\varepsilon_0$, $5\varepsilon_0$ and $2\varepsilon_0$, with a $0.01\,\mathrm m$ middle layer. The first-layer field is $\vec E_1=100\hat x-100\hat y$.

**Find：** Find the fields in layers two and three and the voltage across the middle layer, then explain the field-magnitude differences through polarization.

**Start here：** Split the field into tangential and normal components. At a dielectric interface without free surface charge, preserve tangential electric field and normal displacement, then integrate along the normal to obtain the voltage.

## Part a

Find the electric field in dielectric 2, $\vec E_2$.

### Solution

Use the [electrostatic boundary conditions](formula:boundary) and [constitutive relation](formula:constitutive) from the crib sheet:

$$
D_{1n}-D_{2n}=\rho_s,\quad E_{1t}=E_{2t},\quad\vec{D}_i=\epsilon_i\vec{E}_i
$$

Given $\vec E_1=(100\hat x-100\hat y)\,\mathrm{V/m}$, $\epsilon_1=2\epsilon_0$ and $\epsilon_2=5\epsilon_0$, find $\vec E_2$. With no free surface charge, take $y$ as normal and $x$ as tangential and substitute:

$$
\begin{aligned}E_{2x}&=E_{1x}=100\,\mathrm{V/m},\qquad E_{2z}=0,\\\epsilon_1E_{1y}&=\epsilon_2E_{2y},\\E_{2y}&=\frac{\epsilon_1}{\epsilon_2}E_{1y}=\frac{2\epsilon_0}{5\epsilon_0}(-100)=-40\,\mathrm{V/m}\end{aligned}
$$

Combining the components gives

$$
\vec{E}_2=\boxed{(100\hat{x}-40\hat{y})\,\mathrm{V/m}}
$$

## Part b

Find the electric field in dielectric 3, $\vec E_3$.

### Solution

Apply the crib sheet’s tangential-field and [normal-displacement continuity](formula:boundary) at the second interface:

$$
E_{2t}=E_{3t},\qquad D_{2n}-D_{3n}=0
$$

Given $\vec E_2$ from (a), $\epsilon_2=5\epsilon_0$ and $\epsilon_3=2\epsilon_0$, find $\vec E_3$. Substitute the [constitutive relation](formula:constitutive) into the boundary conditions:

$$
\begin{aligned}E_{3x}&=E_{2x}=100\,\mathrm{V/m},\qquad E_{3z}=0,\\\epsilon_2E_{2y}&=\epsilon_3E_{3y},\\E_{3y}&=\frac{5\epsilon_0}{2\epsilon_0}(-40)=-100\,\mathrm{V/m}\end{aligned}
$$

Combining the components gives

$$
\vec{E}_3=\boxed{(100\hat{x}-100\hat{y})\,\mathrm{V/m}}
$$

## Part c

Calculate the total potential difference across dielectric 2, $V_0$, (i.e. between $y=0.01\,\mathrm{m}$ and $y=0$).

### Solution

Use the [potential-difference integral](formula:potential) from the crib sheet, taking upper potential minus lower potential:

$$
V_0=V(y=0.01)-V(y=0)=-\int_{y=0}^{y=0.01}\vec{E}_2\cdot d\vec{l}
$$

Given the field from (a) and thickness $0.01\,\mathrm m$, find $V_0$. Set $d\vec l=\hat y\,dy$, substitute the dot product and integrate:

$$
\begin{aligned}V_0&=-\int_0^{0.01\,\mathrm m}(100\hat x-40\hat y)\cdot\hat y\,dy\\&=-\int_0^{0.01\,\mathrm m}(-40\,\mathrm{V/m})\,dy\\&=(40\,\mathrm{V/m})[y]_0^{0.01\,\mathrm m}=(40\,\mathrm{V/m})(0.01\,\mathrm m-0)\end{aligned}
$$

This gives a higher potential at the upper surface:

$$
V_0=\boxed{+0.40\,\mathrm V}
$$

## Part d

Using the concept of polarization in dielectric materials, briefly explain why the magnitude of the electric field in dielectric 2 is different from the magnitudes of the electric fields in dielectric 1 and dielectric 3. Be sure to comment on the relative magnitudes: why is one larger/smaller than the others?

### Solution

Use the [boundary conditions](formula:boundary) and [constitutive relation](formula:constitutive) from the crib sheet:

$$
D_{1n}=D_{2n}=D_{3n},\qquad E_{1t}=E_{2t}=E_{3t}
$$

Region 2 has the largest permittivity, $\epsilon_2=5\epsilon_0$. Explain its smaller field by keeping normal $D_n$ and tangential $E_t$ continuous and comparing:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle E_{2n}=\frac{D_n}{5\epsilon_0},\qquad E_{1n}=E_{3n}=\frac{D_n}{2\epsilon_0}\\[4pt]&\displaystyle |E_{2n}|=40<100=|E_{1n}|=|E_{3n}|\quad[\mathrm{V/m}]\\[4pt]&\displaystyle |\vec{E}_2|=\sqrt{100^2+40^2}\approx107.7\,\mathrm{V/m},\quad |\vec{E}_1|=|\vec{E}_3|=\sqrt{100^2+100^2}\approx141.4\,\mathrm{V/m}\end{aligned}
$$

Stronger polarization in region 2 reduces its normal net field, giving

$$
\boxed{|\vec{E}_2|<|\vec{E}_1|=|\vec{E}_3|}
$$

The normal component decreases; do not scale every field component inversely with permittivity.
