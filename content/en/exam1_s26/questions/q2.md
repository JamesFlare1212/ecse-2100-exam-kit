## Question

A transmission line of characteristic impedance $Z_0=50\Omega$ and time delay $T_d=50\mathrm{ps}$ is connected between a generator circuit with voltage $V_g=10\mathrm{V}$ and generator impedance $R_g=30\Omega$, and a load impedance $Z_L=150\Omega$, as shown below. The switch closes at time $t=0$ and provides a DC voltage to the transmission line.

## Guide

This problem distinguishes the transient reflections of a sustained DC step from the eventual steady state.

**Given：** The sustained source has $V_g=10\,\mathrm V$, $R_g=30\,\Omega$, $Z_0=50\,\Omega$ and $R_L=150\,\Omega$, with one-way delay $T_d=50\,\mathrm{ps}$.

**Find：** Find both reflection coefficients and the first traveling waves, plot the specified load-voltage history, and find the steady-state voltage.

**Start here：** Start with the launched wave and reflect it at each arrival time. Load voltage combines incident and reflected waves; the long-time limit should agree with the DC equivalent circuit.

## Part a

Calculate the following quantities:
• The reflection coefficient at the load $\Gamma_L$
• The reflection coefficient at the generator $\Gamma_g$

### Solution

Use the [endpoint reflection coefficient](formula:reflection) from the crib sheet:

$$
\Gamma=\frac{Z-Z_0}{Z+Z_0}
$$

Given $Z_0=50\,\Omega$, $Z_L=150\,\Omega$ and $R_g=30\,\Omega$, find both reflection coefficients. Substitute at each end:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle \Gamma_L=\frac{150-50}{150+50}=\frac{100}{200}=+0.50\\[4pt]&\displaystyle \Gamma_g=\frac{30-50}{30+50}=\frac{-20}{80}=-0.25\end{aligned}
$$

This gives a negative source reflection, indicating voltage inversion:

$$
\Gamma_L=\boxed{0.50},\qquad \Gamma_g=\boxed{-0.25}
$$

## Part b

Calculate numerical values for $V_1^+$, $V_1^-$, $V_2^+$ and $V_2^-$ in the bounce diagram below.

### Solution

Use Lecture 04’s [initial voltage divider](formula:bounce) and the crib sheet’s [reflection relation](formula:reflection):

$$
V_1^+=V_g\frac{Z_0}{R_g+Z_0},\qquad V_n^-=\Gamma_LV_n^+,\qquad V_{n+1}^+=\Gamma_gV_n^-
$$

Given $V_g=10\,\mathrm V$ and $T_d=50\,\mathrm{ps}$, find the four wave amplitudes. Substitute $\Gamma_L=0.5$ and $\Gamma_g=-0.25$ from (a) in propagation order:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle V_1^+=(10\,\mathrm V)\frac{50}{30+50}=10\frac58=6.25\,\mathrm V\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_1^-=\Gamma_LV_1^+=0.5(6.25)=3.125\,\mathrm V\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_2^+=\Gamma_gV_1^-=-0.25(3.125)=-0.78125\,\mathrm V\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_2^-=\Gamma_LV_2^+=0.5(-0.78125)=-0.390625\,\mathrm V\end{aligned}
$$

Label the trajectories at $0$, $50$, $100$ and $150\,\mathrm{ps}$ with these amplitudes:

$$
\begin{gathered}V_1^+=\boxed{6.25\,\mathrm V},\quad V_1^-=\boxed{3.125\,\mathrm V}\\ V_2^+=\boxed{-0.78125\,\mathrm V},\quad V_2^-=\boxed{-0.390625\,\mathrm V}\end{gathered}
$$

## Part c

Sketch a plot of the voltage at the load $V(z=L)$ vs. time for $0\le t\le150\mathrm{ps}$. Label voltage amplitudes and critical times, such as when the voltage wave undergoes relevant reflections.

### Solution

Adding the [arriving wave](formula:bounce) and its [reflected wave](formula:reflection) gives each load-voltage increment:

$$
\Delta V_{L,n}=V_n^++V_n^-=(1+\Gamma_L)V_n^+
$$

Using the wave amplitudes from (b), find the load voltage for $0\le t\le150\,\mathrm{ps}$. Substitute $V_1^+$ and $V_2^+$ into the increment relation and add each increment to the previous level:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle V_L(t)=0\quad(0\le t<50\,\mathrm{ps})\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_L(50^+\,\mathrm{ps})=0+(1+0.5)(6.25)=9.375\,\mathrm V\\[4pt]&\displaystyle \Delta V_{L,2}=(1+0.5)(-0.78125)=-1.171875\,\mathrm V\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_L(150^+\,\mathrm{ps})=9.375-1.171875=8.203125\,\mathrm V\end{aligned}
$$

With load arrivals at $50$ and $150\,\mathrm{ps}$, this gives

$$
\boxed{V_L(t)=\begin{cases}0&0\le t<50\,\mathrm{ps}\\9.375\,\mathrm V&50\le t<150\,\mathrm{ps}\\8.203125\,\mathrm V&t=150^+\,\mathrm{ps}\end{cases}}
$$

## Part d

Calculate the voltage on the transmission line after it has reached steady state $V_{ss}$.

### Solution

At steady DC the lossless line is an ideal connection. Use Kirchhoff’s voltage law:

$$
V_g=I_\infty R_g+I_\infty R_L=I_\infty(R_g+R_L)
$$

Given $V_g=10\,\mathrm V$, $R_g=30\,\Omega$ and $R_L=150\,\Omega$, find $V_\infty$. Solve for current and substitute into the load voltage:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\displaystyle I_\infty=\frac{V_g}{R_g+R_L},\qquad V_\infty=I_\infty R_L=V_g\frac{R_L}{R_g+R_L}\\[4pt]&\displaystyle V_\infty=(10\,\mathrm V)\frac{150}{30+150}=\frac{1500}{180}\,\mathrm V\end{aligned}
$$

Equivalently, substitute the round-trip factor $\Gamma_g\Gamma_L=-1/8$ from (b) into the [bounce series](formula:bounce). Its magnitude is below 1, so the series converges:

$$
V_\infty=9.375\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1/8)^n=\frac{9.375}{1-(-1/8)}
$$

This gives

$$
V_\infty=\frac{25}{3}\,\mathrm V\approx\boxed{8.333\,\mathrm V}
$$
